Overview When fossil fuels are burned in power plants for electricity generation, the largest amount of energy is wasted in the form of heat [1]. Integrating cogeneration or cogeneration (CHP) plants into existing power systems can significantly improve the efficiency of thermal power plants. CHP plants can generate electricity using various fuels and also have the ability to recover and reuse heat, which is often wasted during the power generation process. By utilizing cogeneration equipment to produce both electricity and heat simultaneously, the overall efficiency of the energy conversion process can be increased from 60% to 80% [2] [3]. The growing concern for sustainability has led many countries to integrate CHP units into their existing power systems. This process will save valuable resources and also lead to the economic operation of the power system [4].
The generation of electricity from fossil fuels results in the emission of sulfur dioxide (SO2), nitrogen oxides (NOx), and other greenhouse gases [5]. These gases pollute the air, produce acid rain, and are a primary cause of global warming. Additionally, they pose various health-related issues to humans and have harmful effects on biodiversity and ecosystems. All power companies are compelled to minimize emissions to reduce air pollution and its adverse impacts [6].
In the next section, we will investigate and resolve the available solutions for multi-objective joint economic emission dispatch (CEED), single-objective combined heat and power economic dispatch (CHPED), and multi-objective combined heat and power economic emission dispatch (CHPEED). Among these four formulas, the most challenging issue is the multi-objective CHPEED problem, which is due to conflicting objectives, considering the power-to-heat dependency constraints of CHP units, as well as linear, nonlinear equality and inequality operational constraints. The focus of this study is to find a trade-off solution that is highly complex and challenging for the multi-objective CHPEED problem to reduce the fuel cost and emissions of the power system.
2. Review of literature on combined heat and power economic emission dispatch: Economic dispatch problems aim to find the optimal schedule for generators to minimize the power generation fuel costs subject to power balance constraints and other operational constraints. The ED formula can operate an electric power system economically [7]. To simultaneously minimize the fuel costs and emissions of power plants, a bio-ejected CEED problem is proposed. These objectives not only provide considerable economic benefits but also reduce harmful impacts of polluting gases [5]. There are two methods to solve this highly nonlinear CEED problem using non-convex cost functions. One method is to convert the bio-ejection, multi-dimensional, and highly constrained problem into a single-objective problem and then use an effective random algorithm to solve it. Another method is to use multi-objective algorithms to obtain Pareto optimal (PO) or trade-off solutions because it is impossible to achieve a unique optimal solution satisfying conflicting objectives.
The biological jet CEED optimization problem is converted into a single-objective problem using penalty factors, and then solved using various effective metaheuristic algorithms and their variants. For example, the improved artificial bee colony (ABC) algorithm from [8], the global particle swarm optimization (GPSO) from [9], the chaos-enhanced harmony search algorithm from [10], the pollination algorithm from [11], the biogeographic optimization (GSA) from [12], the gravity search algorithm from [13], the random fractal search algorithm from [14], the multi-region dynamic system's co-evolutionary biosearch algorithm from [15], the fluid mechanism heuristic algorithm from [16], and the lightning algorithm from [17].
Each metaheuristic algorithm used for optimization has its own strengths and weaknesses. To overcome these shortcomings and enhance the advantages of individual algorithms, literature provides hybrid single-objective algorithms to solve this complex CEED problem. These hybrid algorithms combine two different metaheuristic algorithms or combine metaheuristic algorithms with local search techniques to find a trade-off solution to the CEED problem. The artificial bee colony (ABC) algorithm combined with simulated annealing (SA) from [18], the combination of two different metaheuristic algorithms PSO and firefly algorithm from [19], the hybrid firefly and bat algorithm from [20], the hybrid PSO-GSA, as well as other non-conventional methods such as the artificial neural network from [22] have been used in literature to solve the CEED problem. These algorithms provide only a compromise solution in a single run. These algorithms must be run multiple times to obtain a compromise solution. One way to overcome this limitation is to use efficient multi-objective algorithms to generate a compromise solution in a single run.
3. This paper elaborates on a collaborative hybrid metaheuristic algorithm that combines the Non-dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm II (NSGAII) with the Multi-objective Particle Swarm Optimization Algorithm (MOPSO) to address the highly complex economic emission dispatch problem of combined heat and power (CHP), achieving the optimal operation of the power system, reducing environmental pollution impacts. During the iteration process, based on rankings, the overall is divided into two halves. The NSGAII utilizes the upper half of the population for exploration through the use of non-dominated sorting. The modification of the multi-objective Particle Swarm Optimization aims to effectively utilize the lower half of the population by increasing the individual learning coefficient, reducing the global learning coefficient, and employing an adaptive mutation operator. To satisfy linear and nonlinear constraints and ensure that the population always stays within the feasible operating region of the CHP plant, an effective constraint handling mechanism was developed. The proposed hybrid algorithm with an effective constraint handling mechanism enhances search capability through effective information exchange. The algorithm is applied to standard test functions and test systems, considering the valve point effect of thermal power plants, transmission power loss, unit boundaries, and the feasible operating region of CHP units. The hybrid algorithm can obtain a good distribution and diverse Pareto optimal solutions, and it can also converge faster to the actual Pareto optimal front than some existing algorithms. Statistical analysis indicates that the proposed hybrid algorithm is a viable alternative solution for solving this complex and important problem.
